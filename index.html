Overview

RISE studies long-horizon service tasks in which a mobile manipulator must search for objects, navigate between floors, interact with doors and elevators, transport items, and verify the final task state. A long-horizon agentic planner turns the instruction and robot observations into scheduled subtasks. It calls segmentation, candidate-pose, and bird's-eye-view (BEV) tools for spatial understanding; a routing decision then selects navigation or 6D-pose manipulation actions using execution feedback.

The research develops BEV-based geometric reasoning and task-aware grasp evaluation, so choices such as passage poses and grasps account for later stages of the task. For short-lived events, including elevator-door openings, the system prepares event-response hypotheses and monitors relevant states to decide when an action should be safely preempted and the remaining plan repaired. The accompanying benchmark targets complete cross-floor service tasks under varied layouts, passage conditions, and event timing; the videos below illustrate representative executions through synchronized views, keyframes, and execution traces.
